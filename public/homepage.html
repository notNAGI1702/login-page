What is a Forest?

Global Ecosystem Context

A forest is a piece of land with many trees. Forests are essential for the survival of many creatures. There are forests everywhere on the globe, and there are a variety of plants and animals that form an ecosystem.

Rainfall and temperature are the two factors that most affect forests. Woods can be found from the equator to the polar regions. However, the types of forests differ according to climate. Conifers flourish in cold regions, although flowering plants make up most of the forests in temperate and tropical zones. Different rainfall also creates different types of forests. That's why deserts lack forests. The only trees found there are a few spread all over areas where their roots may reach the water below.

Different Types of Forests

Forests can be divided into 5 main categories, depending on the types of trees found in them:

Coniferous Forests

Mostly made up of trees that bear cones, like spruce, pine, fir, and hemlock. Found in northern parts of Asia, Europe, and North America.

Deciduous Forest

Broadleaved trees that shed their leaves between late summer to early autumn turning yellow, red, and orange. Located in seasonal climates.

Mixed Forests

Both coniferous and deciduous tree species mix together. Mostly found across global hilly regions.

Mediterranean Forests

Also known as scrublands or "maquis". Perfect temperature for short oaks, pines, wildflowers, and insect-eating birds.

Tropical Rainforest

Found in hot climates with year-round precipitation. Home to an immense variety of insects, bright birds, and animal species.

Historical Carbonization Note

There was a time in the planet's past where nothing existed that could break down wood. Early trees that fell or died would lay around covering the ground and never rotting away.

As time passed, they got covered with layers and layers of sediment, compressed, and eventually turned into coal. This specific ecological pocket is why coal and other fossil fuels are a completely finite resource—the modern conditions no longer exist for dead wood to transform into coal ever again.
